What Is Bone Marrow Transplant?
Bone Marrow Transplant is a medical procedure in which unhealthy or damaged bone marrow is replaced with healthy blood-forming stem cells. These stem cells help the body produce healthy red blood cells, white blood cells, and platelets.
Conditions Treated with Bone Marrow Transplant
Bone Marrow Transplant may be recommended for selected patients with:
•	Leukemia
•	Lymphoma
•	Multiple Myeloma
•	Thalassemia
•	Aplastic Anemia
•	Sickle Cell Disease
•	Certain immune system disorders
•	Other bone marrow-related conditions
Types of Bone Marrow Transplant
1. Autologous Bone Marrow Transplant
This transplant uses the patient's own stem cells, which are collected and stored before treatment.
2. Allogeneic Bone Marrow Transplant
This procedure uses stem cells from a compatible donor, such as a sibling, family member, or unrelated donor.
3. Haploidentical Bone Marrow Transplant
This transplant uses stem cells from a partially matched donor, commonly a family member.
Bone Marrow Transplant Procedure
The treatment process generally includes:
1.	Detailed medical evaluation and diagnostic tests.
2.	Donor identification and compatibility testing, when required.
3.	Stem cell collection.
4.	Conditioning treatment before transplantation.
5.	Infusion of healthy stem cells.
6.	Post-transplant monitoring and follow-up care.
The exact procedure depends on the patient's diagnosis, medical condition, and transplant type.
